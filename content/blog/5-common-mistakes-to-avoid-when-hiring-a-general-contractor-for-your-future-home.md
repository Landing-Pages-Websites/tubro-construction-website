<p><span>INTRODUCTION: WHAT IS A GENERAL CONTRACTOR AND WHAT DO THEY DO?</span></p>
<h3><span><br/></span></h3>
<p><span>A general contractor is a professional who is responsible for overseeing and coordinating all aspects of a construction project. They are the primary point of contact between the client and the subcontractors, suppliers, and other professionals involved in the project. General contractors provide a wide range of services, from home renovations to new construction projects. They are also responsible for ensuring that all safety standards are met while providing quality construction services at competitive prices. As such, they often act as remodeling contractors to ensure that any changes or additions made to an existing structure adhere to local building codes.</span></p>



<p><span>Hiring the right general contractor is extremely important. This is a big investment in your home and making the right decision will make all the difference. In this article, we will explore 5 common mistakes home owners make when choosing a general contractor.</span></p> <h3><span><br/></span></h3>
<h3><span>1. Not Doing Proper Research &amp; Due Diligence on the Potential Contractors</span></h3>
<h3><span><br/></span></h3>
<p><span><span>When hiring contractors, it is important to do proper research and due diligence to ensure that you are getting the right person for the job. This includes vetting the contractors, researching their background, and checking references. Doing this will help you find a contractor who is qualified and experienced enough to handle your project. Additionally, it will also help you avoid potential problems down the road if things don’t go as planned. It is important to take the time to properly research potential contractors before signing any contracts or agreements with them. <h3><span><br/></span></h3>

Without a contract, there is no legal protection for either party in case of any dispute or misunderstanding.</span></p><p><span><br/></span></p><p><span>Having a clear and detailed agreement in place ensures that both parties understand the expectations and responsibilities of the job.
